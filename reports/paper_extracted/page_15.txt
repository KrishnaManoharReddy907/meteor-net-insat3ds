I. Independent Validation With Radar and
Precipitation-Based Tasks
To further assess the physical credibility of the reconstructed
ﬁelds, we introduce external datasets, including Doppler
weather radar and surface precipitation observations from
national meteorological stations, to independently validate the
temporal super-resolution results.
As illustrated in Fig. 12, two representative severe convec-
tive cases are selected. In each case, the background shows
the FY-4A 10.7-µm brightness temperature ﬁeld, and the
green mask denotes composite radar reﬂectivity exceeding
35 dBZ. Time instants at multiples of 15 min correspond
to the original FY-4A observations, while the intermediate
time steps are the temporally super-resolved reconstructions
produced by METEOR-Net. In both examples, strong radar
echoes exhibit good spatial correspondence with regions of
markedly low brightness temperature, and this relationship is
maintained consistently over time. Moreover, no large areas
of spuriously cold cloud tops are found in regions where no
pronounced radar echoes are present. These results indicate
that, within the AGRI observation framework, METEOR-Net
provides a physically reasonable description of the evolution
of deep convective cloud systems, rather than learning spurious
patterns that contradict the physical laws of atmospheric
convection.
