To evaluate the temporal super-resolution reconstruction
capability of METEOR-Net for convective processes when
smaller local regions are used as input, we conduct a set
of multiscale input experiments. On the test set, three infer-
ence settings are considered: 1) full-patch input, where each
128×128 subregion block is super-resolved as a whole; 2) 1/4
local input, where each 128 × 128 block is divided into four
64× 64 subpatches, which are super-resolved independently
and then stitched back to 128 × 128; and 3) 1/16 local input,
where each 128 × 128 block is divided into 16 32 × 32
subpatches that are likewise super-resolved independently and
then stitched back to 128 × 128.
Quantitative results are reported in Table III. As the input
patch size decreases from 128 × 128 to 32 × 32, SSIM
only slightly decreases from 0.9948 to 0.9940, while RMSE
increases mildly from 0.6816K to 0.7220K. The changes in
CSI and POD at diﬀerent thresholds remain within about 0.01,
and FAR increases by only 0.01–0.02. These results indicate
that METEOR-Net maintains strong quantitative performance
even when inference is performed on smaller local subregions.
All the reconstruction comparison experiments above are
conducted under the ﬁxed data split described. To quantify the
uncertainty of METEOR-Net with respect to diﬀerent dataset
partitions, a ﬁvefold cross-validation over the years 2018–2022
has further been performed. In each fold, one year is used as
the validation set and the remaining four years are used for
training, while the test set is still ﬁxed as 2023 and is never
involved in training or validation. The mean and standard
deviation of each evaluation metric on the test set over the
ﬁve runs are then computed. As summarized in Table IV, the
cross-validation means are very close to the results obtained
with the ﬁxed split, and the standard deviations are extremely
small, indicating that the performance of METEOR-Net is
insensitive to the particular combination of training years and,
from another perspective, supporting the use of the ﬁxed split
in the main experiments.
