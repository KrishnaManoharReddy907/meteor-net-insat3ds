B. Temporal Low-Resolution and High-Resolution Data
Pairs
The FY-4A satellite performs full-disk observations every
15 min to provide full-disk data. This temporal resolution
proves insuﬃcient for monitoring rapid convective processes
and other fast-evolving weather processes. To address this
limitation, FY-4A implements a regional coverage (REGC)
observation mode, which conducts three consecutive obser-
vations within a 15-min interval to provide regional data
equivalent to 5-min temporal resolution. This observation
mode enables high-frequency monitoring of target regions,
providing crucial data support for tracking and warning con-
vective events. However, FY-4A does not operate continuously
in REGC mode. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the satellite alternates
between full-disk and regional observation modes within a 3-h
operational cycle.
To meet the requirement for continuous high temporal
resolution coverage, an optimal approach involves ﬁtting low-
to-high resolution mapping relationships from full-disk data,
thereby enhancing the temporal resolution of full-disk data.
This constitutes the primary objective of the geostationary
satellite temporal super-resolution tasks. The establishment
of such mapping relationships requires explicit construction

absence of high temporal resolution records in full-disk obser-

and high-resolution data pairs from full-disk data becomes
infeasible. To overcome this constraint, a mapping relationship
construction method is proposed. This method leverages the
spatiotemporal consistency between full-disk data and regional
data, using temporal low-resolution and high-resolution data
pairs from regional data to represent full-disk temporal res-
olution mapping relationships. This enables the construction
of low-to-high resolution mapping relationship samples in the
form of data pairs.
Speciﬁcally, the foundation for using regional data in place
of full-disk data to construct data pairs lies in the spatiotempo-
ral consistency between the selected full-disk data and regional
data. Spatially, both regional and full-disk observation modes
use the same AGRI instrument, ensuring continuity and con-
sistency of observational data within identical spatial domains.
This instrumental consistency validates the spatial correlation
between full-disk data and regional data within speciﬁed
latitude–longitude bounds. Temporally, AGRI performs north-
to-south scanning of the globe within each full-disk revisit
period. For the study region located in the mid-to-low latitudes
of the Northern Hemisphere, the scanning time of this area
in full-disk coverage observations mode approximates the
middle of the scanning period. Correspondingly, the inter-
mediate temporal point of regional data during this period
demonstrates strong temporal alignment with full-disk data.
Following this spatiotemporal consistency validation, regional
data at intermediate time points within the study area are
considered spatiotemporally consistent with full-disk data for
each scanning period.
