E. Convective Processes Reconstruction Comparison
Experiment
The 10.7-µm channel of the FY-4A geostationary satellite
is located within an atmospheric window with minimal
absorption, enabling its channel brightness temperature to
eﬀectively represent the cloud-top temperature of convective
clouds. Consequently, this channel is commonly utilized
for the identiﬁcation and tracking of convective clouds.
Thresholds of 273K, 241K, and 221K [50] are applied to the
10.7-µm channel to calculate CSI, POD, and FAR, thereby
quantitatively evaluating the model’s temporal super-
resolution reconstruction performance in cold cloud regions,
convective cloud regions, and strong convective cloud regions.
These quantitative evaluation results are used to indicate the
model’s performance in reconstructing convective processes.
Fig. 9 illustrates the variation in CSI, POD, and FAR across
these three brightness temperature thresholds for six tem-
poral super-resolution reconstruction methods, with metrics
averaged across all test samples. METEOR-Net consistently
achieves superior CSI and POD values across all thresholds
while maintaining low FAR, and exhibits notably slower
degradation with decreasing brightness temperature thresholds
compared to alternative methods. This performance charac-
teristic indicates robust capability of METEOR-Net in ﬁtting
rapid convective evolution processes within convective and
strong convective cloud regions. While competing methods
achieve comparable CSI and POD values at the 273K thresh-
old, their performance deteriorates rapidly at lower thresholds,
suggesting limited capability in modeling nonlinear processes
such as convective cloud generation, development, merger, and
dissipation within convective regions.
