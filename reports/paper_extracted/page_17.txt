V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this article, a temporal super-resolution framework
for geostationary satellite data based on spatiotemporal
self-supervised learning is proposed, addressing limitations
in conventional VFI frameworks through comprehensive
improvements in data construction, model architecture, and
training-evaluation strategy. In addition, a novel model,
METEOR-Net, is introduced for temporal super-resolution of
geostationary satellite cloud data. METEOR-Net is a ﬂow-
based temporal super-resolution method that replaces optical
ﬂow-based frame interpolation by estimating and evolving
temporal-ﬂows, thereby better adapting to the data charac-
teristics of geostationary satellites. Within this framework,
extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments indicate that
METEOR-Net yields temporal super-resolution reconstruc-
tion results that surpass existing temporal super-resolution
methods and models for geostationary satellites in terms
of visual ﬁdelity and the accuracy of convective process
reconstruction. Regional transfer generalization tests, together
with additional experiments over subregions with diﬀerent
terrain and climatic conditions and small-sample regional ﬁne-
tuning, provide a comprehensive assessment of the model’s
regional generalization ability and indicate good potential
for regional transferability. Although an exhaustive evaluation
across all possible climate zones has not yet been conducted,
these regional experiments suggest a practically feasible usage
pattern: ﬁrst, pretrain a general model over a broad domain,
and then perform lightweight ﬁne-tuning with a small number
of local samples in the target region, thereby striking a balance
between computational cost and regional adaptability.
In addition, an interpretability analysis of METEOR-Net’s
temporal super-resolution reconstruction process is conducted.
