Third, training and evaluation strategy. The goals of geosta-
tionary satellite temporal super-resolution diﬀer fundamentally
from VFI goals, requiring adapted training objectives and
evaluation metrics. While video interpolation aims to gen-
erate multiple intermediate frames for visual smoothness,
satellite temporal super-resolution focuses on reconstructing
unobserved convective evolution processes, requiring inter-
mediate frames to serve as reliable anchors for critical
physical transitions. The sparse spatial distribution of con-
vective regions poses challenges during model training, as
these crucial features risk being smoothed out. Therefore,
extra attention must be given to convective regions during
the training of geostationary satellite temporal super-resolution
models. Furthermore, image reconstruction metrics used in
VFI frameworks inadequately quantify model performance in
reconstructing convective processes, highlighting the need for
specialized evaluation metrics focused on convective processes
reconstruction capabilities.
To address these challenges, we propose a novel spatiotem-
poral self-supervised learning framework for geostationary
satellite temporal super-resolution. This framework enhances
the VFI techniques previously adopted in geostationary
satellite cloud data temporal super-resolution tasks through
comprehensive improvements in data construction, model
architecture, and training-evaluation strategy. Speciﬁcally, we
propose multichannel encoding and temporal-ﬂow evolution
network for optimized-observation reconstruction (METEOR-
Net), a network that maintains the advantages of ﬂow-based
methods while eliminating dependence on optical ﬂow to
mitigate the incompatibility issues of ﬂow-based approaches
with geostationary satellite cloud data. The main contributions
are as follows.
1) The spatiotemporal consistency between regional obser-
vation and full-disk observation of geostationary satel-
lites is used to construct reliable temporal low-resolution
and high-resolution data pairs. This method enables
the formation of ground truth labels for spatiotemporal
self-supervised learning constrained by spatiotemporal
consistency relationships, eliminating the requirement
for additional annotations.
2) METEOR-Net is proposed as a novel architecture
that goes beyond previous ﬂow-based methods through
temporal sequence-ﬂow modeling. The network intro-
duces three synergistic modules speciﬁcally designed
to address the challenges of multichannel asynchrony
and complementarity, nonlinear dynamics, and the
accompanying occlusion modeling.
3) A convective region-weighted loss function is pro-
posed for training the model to make additional focus
on the convective regions. Furthermore, meteorological
evaluation metrics targeting convective process recon-
struction are introduced to evaluate the model’s ability to
reconstruct convective processes.
4) Experimental results on the FY-4A satellite cloud data
temporal super-resolution reconstruction task demon-
strate that METEOR-Net outperforms existing temporal
super-resolution methods for geostationary satellite
data in both visual ﬁdelity and convective process
reconstruction accuracy, establishing it as the current
state-of-the-art (SOTA) method.
II. D ATA
A. Introduction to FY-4A Satellite Cloud Data
The geostationary satellite cloud data used in this study
is acquired from FY-4A, China’s next-generation geostation-
ary satellite launched in December 2016 [7]. The Advanced
Geostationary Radiation Imager (AGRI) onboard FY-4A cap-
tures data across 14 spectral channels, spanning visible,
near-infrared, mediumwave infrared, water vapor, and long-
wave infrared. This comprehensive spectral coverage enables
monitoring of Earth’s atmosphere, cloud systems, oceans, and
land surface characteristics.
The study region encompasses central and eastern China
(20.0◦ N–40.0◦ N, 104.0◦ E–125.0◦ E), where convective
weather events occur frequently. This region is selected
to ensure the temporal super-resolution results encompass
numerous rapid convective processes for model validation.
