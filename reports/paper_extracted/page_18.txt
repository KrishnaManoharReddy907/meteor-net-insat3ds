This study improves the temporal resolution of FY-4A
cloud data from 15 to 5 min, which alleviates the temporal
sparsity of geostationary measurements and provides higher
temporal-resolution cloud observation sequences for future
convective weather identiﬁcation and warning. Case analyses
of convective evolution show that METEOR-Net reconstructs
the cloud-top cooling evolution associated with early deep
convection formation within the 15 min observation gap.
Under more challenging rapidly developing conditions, it also
captures the formation location and timing of the very low
brightness temperature core and its subsequent displacement.
Independent validation based on radar reﬂectivity con-
sistency checks and station-precipitation-based downstream
identiﬁcation and prediction tasks further indicates that the
reconstructed ﬁelds exhibit good spatiotemporal correspon-
dence with regions of intense radar echoes, do not produce
widespread spurious cold cloud tops, and provide more phys-
ically meaningful information for downstream applications.
Although the temporal super-resolution model performs
well in reconstruction experiments, we ﬁnd that its reconstruc-
tion errors remain nonnegligible for operational applications.
The multichannel error maps in the experiments show that
large errors tend to concentrate in regions with rapid evolution.
This challenge is most pronounced during convective initiation
and rapid development, when cloud systems exhibit strong
nonlinearity and high temporal variability, which makes inter-
mediate frame reconstruction more diﬃcult. During initiation,
the cold cloud-top signal can be weak and conﬁned to a
small fraction of pixels, which increases sensitivity to small
perturbations. During rapid development, fast nonlinear evolu-
tion, and displacement can increase the risk of spatiotemporal
biases.
Based on these characteristics, we recommend com-
bining the 5-min super-resolved frames with the original
FY-4A observations at the 15-min anchor times to perform
temporal-consistency checks and quality control, with partic-
ular attention to the propagation of reconstruction errors into
downstream applications. For example, convective identiﬁca-
tion can suﬀer from missed detections or false alarms due
to biases in cold cloud-top location or intensity, and short-
term precipitation nowcasting can also be aﬀected. For critical
operational applications, it is further advisable to incorporate
uncertainty-aware safeguards, including multisource informa-
tion fusion such as radar and numerical weather prediction
(NWP), multimodel or ensemble consistency checks, and
conﬁdence-based quality control, to ﬂag and manage low-
conﬁdence reconstructions.
Finally, in this study, METEOR-Net is instantiated to per-
form temporal super-resolution from 15 to 5 min within each
full-disk revisit interval. Speciﬁcally, two intermediate frames
at T + 5 min and T + 10 min are reconstructed using 5-min
FY-4A/AGRI REGC observations as temporal high-resolution
ground truth labels. Nevertheless, the sequence-ﬂow-based
architecture itself is not intrinsically restricted to a ﬁxed
temporal step and can, in principle, be extended via time con-
ditioning to interpolate intermediate frames at arbitrary time
points within the revisit window. At present, this capability
cannot be rigorously validated on FY-4A because the available
ground truth observations have a temporal resolution of only
5 min. The next-generation geostationary satellite FY-4B,
however, can provide regional observations with a temporal
resolution of 1 min. In future work, we plan to migrate
the proposed temporal super-resolution framework to FY-4B
data and to conduct more stringent observational validation of
intermediate reconstructions at ﬁner time resolutions, further
assessing the physical ﬁdelity of METEOR-Net’s arbitrary
time interpolation for rapidly evolving convective processes.
